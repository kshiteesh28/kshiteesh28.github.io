## The problem

A rate limiter sits at a difficult boundary: it protects a finite service while deciding which requests get through. A concurrent or distributed implementation also has to keep its quota decisions consistent.

I built RateGuard to work on that boundary, examining the tradeoff between fairness and throughput rather than treating admission as a simple counter.

## My contribution

I built the distributed rate limiter using C++, gRPC, Protocol Buffers, Redis Cluster and etcd. The project also uses Prometheus and Docker. I fixed a race condition that caused quota overages by implementing atomic compare-and-swap.

The diagram above is a conceptual view of admission control. It illustrates the problem, not a complete deployment topology.

## The important design decision

**Quota updates need a clear atomic boundary.** Concurrent requests cannot safely make independent decisions against a stale view of the same budget. My compare-and-swap change addressed the race responsible for overages.

Fairness and throughput were also part of the project’s tradeoff analysis. Giving a busy client first access can consume capacity before quieter clients make progress. A more balanced allocation can redistribute that access. The right policy depends on the service’s contract.

## Results and validation

My project record reports a **58% reduction in API rejection rate** and elimination of quota violations after the atomicity fix. These are project-specific results. The record does not include a public benchmark dataset, load profile or reproducible measurement report, so I do not present them as general performance guarantees.

The race condition and resulting quota overages are the documented correctness issue addressed by the implementation.
